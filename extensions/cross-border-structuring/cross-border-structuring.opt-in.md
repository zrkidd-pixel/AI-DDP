# Cross-Border Structuring (opt-in)

Activates when: the target has meaningful non-US revenue, operations, or
ownership. Read by Legal & Structuring, Financing/Capital Structure, and
Underwriting in addition to their normal rules.

## Tax structuring considerations

- **Repatriation constraints.** Cash sitting in a foreign subsidiary may not
  be freely available to service US-based acquisition debt or fund a US
  sponsor's return — check withholding tax on dividends/distributions from
  each relevant jurisdiction and whether tax treaties reduce it.
- **Transfer pricing.** If the target has intercompany transactions across
  borders (shared services, IP licensing, financing), existing transfer
  pricing positions carry audit risk that survives a change of control —
  flag to Legal & Structuring for counsel review, don't assume it transfers
  cleanly.
- **Holding structure.** Where the acquisition vehicle sits (which
  jurisdiction) affects treaty access, exit taxation, and financing
  deductibility — a structuring decision for counsel and tax advisors, not
  something this framework resolves on its own.

## Regulatory approval

- **Cross-border M&A approval regimes.** Beyond standard antitrust review,
  check whether the target's jurisdiction(s) or the buyer's jurisdiction
  have a foreign-investment review process (e.g., a national-security-style
  screening of foreign or foreign-backed acquirers) that could delay or
  block closing. This is a real timeline and certainty-of-close risk, not
  a formality.
- **Sector-specific foreign-ownership limits.** Some sectors (media,
  telecom, defense-adjacent, critical infrastructure) cap foreign
  ownership percentages in specific countries — confirm this doesn't
  constrain the deal structure before it's underwritten.

## Underwriting adjustments

- **FX exposure.** If a meaningful share of revenue or costs is in a
  non-USD currency, underwriting should show the deal's sensitivity to FX
  movement, not just a spot-rate conversion presented as if it were static.
- **Local leverage market.** Financing terms available in the target's home
  jurisdiction may differ materially from `underwriting/financing-
  benchmarks.md`, which is not scoped to any specific non-US market —
  source local benchmarks separately rather than assuming US terms
  transfer.
- **Country risk premium.** If the target's primary operations are in a
  market with material sovereign or currency risk, that risk needs to show
  up somewhere explicit (a premium adjustment or a stated risk factor) —
  not silently absorbed into a standard domestic discount rate assumption.

## What this extension does not do

Does not give tax or legal advice, does not determine deal structure, and
does not replace qualified local counsel and tax advisors in every relevant
jurisdiction. Its job is making sure these considerations get raised at the
right time, not resolving them.
