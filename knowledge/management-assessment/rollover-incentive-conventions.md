# Rollover & Incentive Conventions

Referenced by: Management Assessment Agent, Legal & Structuring Agent

## Rollover equity

Rollover equity is the portion of a seller's (often management's) proceeds
reinvested into the post-close capital structure rather than cashed out at
close. It is not a fixed convention — the appropriate rollover percentage
depends on deal size, the seller's liquidity needs, and how much ongoing
alignment the sponsor wants. **Do not assume a specific rollover percentage as
a default; the classroom version of this framework explicitly assumed "no
rollover equity" as a simplifying assumption — a real deal should state its
rollover assumption explicitly and source it from the actual negotiation, not
carry the training assumption forward.**

Effects of rollover on the model:
- Reduces the sponsor equity check dollar-for-dollar against the rolled amount.
- Aligns management with post-close performance, since their proceeds are now
  tied to the same exit outcome as the sponsor.
- Changes the effective ownership/control structure — confirm with the Legal &
  Structuring Agent whether rollover triggers different governance or
  minority-protection terms.

## Management equity incentive pools

A typical buyout creates a new management incentive pool (sometimes called a
"promote" or option pool) sized as a percentage of the post-close equity,
often structured with vesting tied to time and/or performance milestones.
There is no single universal pool size — it varies by deal size, sector norms,
and how much of the value-creation plan depends on retained management vs. a
new operating team brought in by the sponsor.

## Vesting and performance conditions

- **Time-based vesting:** retention-oriented, typically vesting over the
  expected hold period.
- **Performance-based vesting (often tied to a return threshold, e.g., the
  sponsor achieving a minimum MOIC or IRR on exit):** aligns management
  incentive directly with fund returns, but only pays out if the deal succeeds
  — consider whether this appropriately shares downside risk too, or only
  upside.
- **Acceleration on change of control / termination:** confirm what happens to
  unvested equity if the company is sold again, or if a manager is terminated
  without cause — a poorly structured provision here can create a
  hard-to-unwind conflict later.

## Handoff

This agent proposes the incentive structure's mechanics and flags alignment
gaps; Legal & Structuring turns it into actual contractual terms; final
economics are a negotiated, partner-level decision — this file documents
convention and tradeoffs, not a number to plug in automatically.
