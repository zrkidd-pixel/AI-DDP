# Deal Structure Decision Tree

Referenced by: Legal & Structuring Agent, Underwriting Agent

This is a framework for surfacing the right questions, not a substitute for
actual legal advice — every real deal needs qualified counsel to finalize
structure. This agent's job is to flag which considerations apply and route
them to counsel with the right context, not to make the final structuring call.

## Asset deal vs. stock (equity) deal

- **Asset deal:** buyer acquires specific assets and (typically) assumes only
  specified liabilities. Generally allows a step-up in tax basis on acquired
  assets (potential depreciation/amortization benefit to the buyer) and lets
  the buyer leave unwanted liabilities behind — but requires re-assigning
  contracts, licenses, and permits individually, which can be slow and may
  require third-party consents.
- **Stock deal:** buyer acquires the target entity itself, including all its
  assets, contracts, and liabilities (known and unknown) by operation of law.
  Simpler to execute (no individual contract reassignment) but the buyer
  inherits historical liabilities — which is exactly why diligence quality
  (financial, legal, commercial) matters more, not less, in a stock deal.
- **Which one applies often depends on factors outside pure preference:** the
  target's legal form, tax considerations for the seller (sellers often prefer
  stock deals for tax reasons), lender requirements, and whether key contracts
  contain change-of-control or assignment restrictions that make one structure
  materially more expensive or slower than the other.

## Successor liability flag

Even in an asset deal, certain liabilities (environmental, some employment
and pension obligations, product liability in some jurisdictions) can
transfer to the buyer under successor-liability doctrines regardless of deal
structure. Flag this for counsel review rather than assuming an asset deal
fully insulates the buyer.

## Key questions to route to counsel before finalizing structure

- Does the target have material contracts with change-of-control consent or
  termination rights that a stock deal would trigger anyway (many do)?
- Is there a tax-driven preference from the seller side that conflicts with
  the buyer's preferred structure, and what's the cost of bridging it (e.g.,
  a 338(h)(10)-style election in the US, where applicable)?
- Are there licenses, permits, or government contracts that don't transfer
  automatically under an asset deal?
- What jurisdiction-specific successor-liability exposure applies?

## What this agent does not do

Does not draft or finalize legal structure, does not give tax advice, and
does not substitute for transaction counsel. Its output is a structured list
of considerations and open questions for counsel, tied to the specific facts
of this target — not a generic structure recommendation.
