# Purchase Agreement Glossary

Referenced by: Legal & Structuring Agent, Thesis / IC Memo Agent (for the
risk section)

Reference terms for tracking key commercial points in a draft purchase
agreement — not a substitute for counsel's review of actual contract language.

- **Representations & warranties (reps & warranties):** seller statements of
  fact about the business (financial statements are accurate, no undisclosed
  litigation, title to assets is clean, etc.) that the buyer relies on. A
  breach can trigger indemnification.
- **Indemnification cap:** the maximum amount a seller is obligated to pay the
  buyer for a breach of reps/warranties — often a percentage of purchase
  price, sometimes carved out entirely for certain "fundamental"
  representations (e.g., title, capitalization) which may carry a higher or
  uncapped exposure.
- **Basket / deductible:** a minimum threshold of losses before indemnification
  claims can be made at all (a "true deductible" means only losses above the
  basket are recoverable; a "tipping basket" means once the threshold is
  crossed, all losses including the first dollar become recoverable).
- **Survival period:** how long after closing a representation remains
  enforceable — general reps typically survive for a defined period post-
  close; fundamental reps (title, capitalization, tax) often survive longer
  or indefinitely.
- **Representations & warranties insurance (RWI):** a buyer-side (usually)
  insurance policy that covers breaches of seller reps/warranties, often used
  in competitive processes to reduce the seller's indemnification exposure
  and make an offer more attractive — changes the negotiation dynamic around
  caps and baskets since the policy, not the seller, bears much of the risk.
- **Working capital adjustment / peg:** a post-close true-up mechanism
  comparing actual closing working capital to an agreed "normal" target (the
  peg) — see `financial-diligence/qoe-redflag-checklist.md` on establishing
  the peg from a proper trend analysis, not a single point-in-time balance.
- **Earnout:** contingent purchase-price consideration paid post-close if the
  business hits specified performance milestones — shifts some valuation risk
  to the seller but creates ongoing measurement disputes and potential
  misalignment if the seller no longer controls the business's operating
  decisions during the earnout period.
- **MAC / MAE clause (Material Adverse Change/Effect):** a condition allowing
  the buyer to walk away (or renegotiate) if a significant adverse event
  affects the target between signing and closing — heavily negotiated, narrow
  in practice, rarely successfully invoked.
- **Escrow:** a portion of purchase price held by a third party post-close as
  security for indemnification claims, released after a defined period absent
  a claim.

## Practical rule for this agent

Track these terms as they appear in drafts and flag any that deviate
materially from what was assumed in the underwriting model (e.g., a working
capital peg set very differently from the QoE-derived normal level, or an
indemnification cap far below the model's assumed risk exposure) — a legal
term that quietly changes the deal's economics should surface back to
Underwriting, not stay siloed in the legal workstream.
