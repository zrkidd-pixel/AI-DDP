# Competitive Positioning Frameworks

Referenced by: Commercial Diligence Agent, Thesis / IC Memo Agent

## Structuring the competitive landscape

- **Direct competitors:** companies selling a substantially similar product to
  the same buyer, at a comparable price point.
- **Indirect / substitute competitors:** different products solving the same
  underlying customer need — often the more dangerous long-term threat because
  they don't show up in a naive "who else sells this" search.
- **Market share estimation:** prefer bottom-up estimates (target revenue ÷
  bottom-up market size from `market-intelligence/market-sizing-methodology.md`)
  over a top-down share claimed in a single research report, and always state
  the denominator's scope explicitly — "share of X" is meaningless without
  defining X.

## Five forces, applied practically (not as an academic exercise)

- **Buyer power:** does the customer base have real switching alternatives and
  low switching costs, or is there lock-in (integration cost, contractual
  terms, data/workflow embedding)?
- **Supplier power:** is the target dependent on a concentrated supplier base
  or a scarce input, and what happens to margin if that supplier raises
  prices?
- **Threat of new entrants:** what's the actual barrier to a well-funded new
  entrant — capital intensity, regulatory approval, brand, distribution
  access, or network effects? A barrier that's "we were first" is not
  durable; a barrier that's structural (regulatory license, patented
  technology, exclusive distribution) is.
- **Threat of substitutes:** see indirect competitors above.
- **Competitive rivalry:** is the sector rational (players compete on
  differentiation) or is it a price-competitive commodity dynamic that
  compresses margin over time?

## Connecting this to the investment thesis

Competitive positioning findings should directly inform two thesis
components: the **consolidation opportunity** (is this sector fragmented with
sub-scale competitors that a platform could roll up — see
`value-creation/value-lever-taxonomy.md`) and the **key risk factors** (is the
target's position defensible against the specific threats identified, or is
this a business whose current margin/multiple assumes a competitive position
that's actually eroding). A competitive analysis that doesn't change either
the thesis or the risk section hasn't actually been used.

## Sourcing discipline

Market share and competitive claims follow the same tiering as
`market-intelligence/source-credibility-hierarchy.md` — a competitor's own
marketing claims about their market position are Tier 4 at best (self-
reported) and should never be repeated as fact without independent
corroboration.
