# Customer Concentration Frameworks

Referenced by: Commercial Diligence Agent, Financial Diligence / QoE Agent

This is the target-specific counterpart to Market & Sector Intelligence: where
that agent looks at the industry, this one looks at whether *this company's*
revenue base is durable.

## Core metrics to compute

- **Revenue concentration:** % of revenue from top 1, top 5, and top 10
  customers. There is no universal "safe" threshold — it depends on contract
  length, switching cost, and sector norms — but any single customer above
  roughly 10% of revenue deserves explicit diligence into contract terms and
  relationship history, and concentration in the top 5–10 above roughly a
  third of revenue is a flag worth surfacing regardless of sector.
- **Logo retention rate:** % of customers (by count) retained year over year.
  Distinct from revenue retention — a business can lose many small customers
  while retaining nearly all revenue, or vice versa.
- **Net revenue retention (NRR):** revenue from the existing customer base this
  period vs. last period, including expansion, contraction, and churn, before
  counting new-customer revenue. NRR above 100% means the existing base is
  growing on its own even before new sales.
- **Contract structure:** term length, auto-renewal provisions, termination-
  for-convenience clauses, and whether contracts survive a change of control
  (some contracts include change-of-control consent or termination rights that
  a buyer needs to know about before signing, not after).

## Red flags

- Revenue growth driven by a small number of large, recent customer wins with
  no track record of retention yet.
- Informal or handshake relationships underlying a meaningful revenue share —
  route the earnings-quality implication to
  `financial-diligence/qoe-redflag-checklist.md`.
- Customer concentration increasing over time (a sign the business is
  becoming more dependent on fewer relationships, not less).
- A single customer relationship tied to a specific individual at the target
  (e.g., a founder's personal relationship) rather than an institutional
  relationship that survives a management change.

## Primary research (where this data actually comes from)

Concentration percentages are usually available from the target's own
financials or data room disclosures. Retention, satisfaction, and
switching-cost color typically require primary research — customer reference
calls, expert-network conversations, win/loss interviews with the sales team.
**Conducting these interviews is a human function; this agent's job is to
structure the questions, synthesize the findings, and flag what still needs a
human conversation to resolve** — not to fabricate customer sentiment from
secondary sources.
