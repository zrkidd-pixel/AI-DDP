# Market Sizing Methodology

Referenced by: Market & Sector Intelligence Agent, Thesis / IC Memo Agent

## Top-down vs. bottom-up

- **Top-down:** start from a total addressable market figure (from a Tier 2–3
  source per `source-credibility-hierarchy.md`) and narrow by the target's
  specific segment, geography, and customer type. Fast, but inherits whatever
  scoping error the source estimate carries.
- **Bottom-up:** build the market size from unit economics — number of
  potential customers × price point × purchase frequency, or number of
  competitors × their disclosed or estimated revenue. Slower, but more
  defensible because every input is independently checkable.

**Prefer bottom-up when the target's specific niche is narrow enough that
top-down estimates would be scoped too broadly to be useful** — this was
necessary, for example, when a sector's published market-size estimates
ranged from roughly $800B to $1.9T depending on what the research firm
included, making a single top-down number nearly meaningless for a narrow
sub-segment.

## Reporting a range, not a false-precision point estimate

When multiple Tier 2–3 sources disagree (which they usually will), report the
range and cite each source, rather than averaging them into a single number
that implies more precision than actually exists. State explicitly *why* the
estimates differ (different category scope is the most common reason) rather
than leaving the disagreement unexplained.

## Growth rate sourcing

A CAGR forecast is only as good as its stated time horizon and base year —
always report both. A "high single-digit CAGR through 2032" from a source
published in 2024 is not the same claim as one published in 2026; note the
publication date of any forecast.

## Industry structure and fragmentation

Beyond total size, characterize how the market is actually structured:
concentration (a few dominant players vs. a long tail of small operators),
recent consolidation activity, and where the target's specific candidate set
sits in that structure. A screen's own findings are valid evidence here — if a
systematic sweep for candidates turned up a bifurcated population (a handful
of mega-caps and many sub-scale micro-caps with little in between), that's a
real, citable structural finding about the market, sourced from the screen
itself `[calc: screening output]`, not just from third-party research.

## Regulatory environment checklist

For any sector, check at minimum: recent or pending legislation specific to
the product category, ongoing regulatory-agency enforcement activity or
guidance, and any overlapping compliance regimes (e.g., state-by-state privacy
law patchworks) that raise cost or diligence burden for an acquirer. Source
each from a Tier 1 source (the statute, the agency report) wherever possible,
not a secondhand summary of it.
