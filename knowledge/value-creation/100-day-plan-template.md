# 100-Day Plan Template

Referenced by: Value Creation / 100-Day Planning Agent, Monitoring Agent (this
plan is the baseline Monitoring measures actual performance against)

## Structure

1. **Day-one priorities (weeks 1–2):** governance setup (board composition,
   reporting cadence agreed with management), confirming the management
   retention/incentive plan is actually in place (not still being negotiated
   post-close), and establishing the KPI baseline the Monitoring Agent will
   track going forward — see `monitoring/kpi-dashboard-templates.md`.
2. **Quick wins (weeks 2–6):** value-creation levers identified in
   `value-creation/value-lever-taxonomy.md` that are low-difficulty and
   high-confidence — things that don't require new capital or capability and
   can demonstrate early momentum to the board and management team.
3. **Structural initiatives (weeks 6–14):** levers requiring more setup —
   procurement consolidation, pricing initiatives, initial bolt-on outreach
   if a buy-and-build thesis is part of the plan. Each initiative needs a
   named owner (not "the company will..."), a rough sizing, and a checkpoint
   date.
4. **Diligence follow-ups:** any item flagged during Financial, Commercial, or
   Management diligence that wasn't fully resolved pre-close should have an
   explicit owner and resolution date in the 100-day plan — an open diligence
   flag that disappears after close without being tracked is a gap, not a
   resolved issue.

## What must NOT be vague

Every initiative in the plan needs: an owner, a rough dollar or percentage
impact with a stated basis, and a checkpoint date. "Explore margin
improvement opportunities" is not a 100-day plan item; "renegotiate the top
three supplier contracts, targeting X% procurement savings, owned by [role],
checkpoint at day 60" is.

## Handoff to Monitoring

The 100-day plan's initiatives and their sizing become the baseline that
`monitoring/kpi-dashboard-templates.md` tracks actual results against. If the
plan changes materially after close (a lever turns out not to work, a new one
emerges), that change should be an explicit, dated revision to this document
— not a silent drift where the original plan is quietly abandoned without
anyone recording why.
