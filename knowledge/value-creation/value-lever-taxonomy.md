# Value Creation Lever Taxonomy

Referenced by: Value Creation / 100-Day Planning Agent, Thesis / IC Memo Agent

## The four standard lever categories

- **Revenue growth:** organic (pricing, new product lines, channel expansion,
  geographic expansion) and inorganic (bolt-on acquisitions). Distinguish
  levers the current management team can execute from ones that require new
  capabilities or leadership.
- **Margin / cost improvement:** procurement savings, shared-services
  consolidation (especially relevant when multiple owned/licensed brands or
  bolt-ons share back-office functions), headcount and overhead
  rationalization, pricing discipline. Be specific about *why* the margin gap
  exists today (e.g., sub-scale procurement, redundant back-office across
  brands) rather than asserting a generic "margin expansion opportunity."
- **Multiple arbitrage (buy-and-build):** acquiring smaller, sub-scale
  competitors at lower entry multiples and integrating them under the
  platform, with the combined entity valued at the platform's (typically
  higher) multiple at exit. This is the direct value-creation expression of a
  fragmented-market consolidation thesis identified in Market Intelligence and
  Competitive Positioning — if the thesis says the sector is fragmented, this
  lever should have a specific, named pipeline of plausible bolt-on
  candidates, not just the assertion that fragmentation exists.
- **Working capital and capital efficiency:** improving cash conversion cycle,
  renegotiating supplier/customer payment terms, reducing excess inventory —
  frees cash without requiring operational transformation.

## Connecting recurring/connected-revenue shifts to value creation

Where the underlying business shifts revenue mix toward recurring or
subscription-like models (a distinct commercial lever worth calling out on
its own), that shift itself often supports a higher exit multiple
independent of any margin or growth improvement — because recurring revenue
is priced by the market as more durable than one-time sales. If this lever
applies, it should tie directly to the specific competitive positioning and
customer-concentration findings, not be asserted generically.

## What makes a lever real vs. a slide-ware assumption

A lever belongs in the thesis only if it has: (1) a specific mechanism (not
"improve margins" but "consolidate sourcing across the three owned brands to
capture volume discounts currently unavailable to any single brand"), (2) a
rough sizing with a stated basis, and (3) an owner and rough timeline once
in the 100-day plan. A lever without these three things is a hope, not a plan.

## Sequencing and prioritization

Not every lever should be pursued simultaneously. Prioritize by a rough
impact-vs-difficulty view: levers that are high-impact and executable with
the existing team and capital structure come first; levers that require new
capital, new leadership, or unresolved diligence questions (e.g., an
unconfirmed bolt-on pipeline) belong later in the plan, not in the first
100 days.
